P1: Computer Vision in Medical Imaging

Computer vision is a field of machine learning that mostly deals with image and video analysis.

In medical imaging technology, computer vision has been used successfully to greatly enhance the capabilities of medical imaging systems, like in computed tomography(CT), magnetic resonance imaging(MRI) and more. In general, this has massively improved the accuracy and efficiency of providing medical diagnoses from these imaging systems, reduced the workload of medical professionals, and opened up new possibilities for analyzing medical imaging data in real time, like in live surgical guidance.

Case Study: Vision-based Behaviour Monitoring in Autism Care

Traditionally, behaviour monitoring for autism-spectrum-discorder (ASD) relies heavily on wearable biosensors, but up to 10% of patients reject these devices due to sensory sensitivity, leaving caregivers without critical data for intervention planning.

Researchers at Northeastern University developed a computer-vision-driven behavior analysis system that uses RGB cameras and multi-object tracking (MOT) to monitor aggressive or self-injurious behaviors without requiring any wearable devices, which consisted of:

  • Multi-object tracking (MOT) to simultaneously follow patients and staff
  • Pose estimation to extract skeletal keypoints to detect non-standard postures like crawling or curling
  • Action recognition to identify dysregulated behaviors
  • Behavior classification models trained specifically on ASD patterns
